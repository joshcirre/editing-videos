### Cutting

Covers where and why to cut: phrases, retakes, fillers, jump cuts, cameras, zooms, exact frames.
Everything here is a default that the person's profile or words override, except what's marked
`(hard rule)`: see [rules.md](rules.md).

- **Every cut has a reason**: it takes out dead time or a mistake, changes what we learn, or
  follows an action.
- **Cut between phrases** (what's said in one breath), in silence, never inside a word (hard rule).
  Leave a breath before the next word: about 0.1–0.2 s (house) on the incoming side, a little
  more after the end of a sentence. How long the whole pause stays is in [pace.md](pace.md).
  A profile can differ: measured on one creator's own cuts of two tutorials, about
  0.35 s was kept after the outgoing word and 0.08 s before the incoming one, and pieces were
  rarely under 5 s and never under 3 s (measured).
- **Transcript times are estimates, not cut points.** (hard rule) A word's last sound (a final "t"
  or "s") often runs past its transcribed end, and a quiet first consonant starts before its
  transcribed start. Place each cut by the sound (the waveform or levels), and check every join on
  both sides: the last word out is whole, the first word in is whole, and the pause between reads as
  one thought.
- **The first word in starts the clip.** Where one take joins another, the incoming side begins
  on the first sound that's meant: drop the breath before a restart, the mouth reset and any
  scrap of the take before. A few idle frames after a visible cut already feel like a stumble.
  Breaths inside a sentence that carries on stay.
- **Retakes and fillers are decisions, not a filter.** Follow the two paths below for each one.
- **Jump cuts** on one camera: cover them with another angle, B-roll, or a punch-in that clearly
  changes the size (15% (common) or more). Fast formats can leave them bare; slow ones shouldn't.
- **Sound leads and lingers**: let the next voice start under the end of a shot (a J-cut), or a
  voice carry over into the next shot (an L-cut), so cuts between people and places feel natural.
- **Several cameras**: the picture follows whoever is talking, holds reactions that matter, and
  never cuts to the same framing mid-sentence.
- **Zooms and punch-ins** need a reason (emphasis, a detail to see). Never across a cut, and not
  on every line. A slow drift goes back to its start only on a cut; a reset in the middle of a
  shot is a bump.
- **A cut is where the picture changes, to the frame.** (hard rule) Don't trust cut times carried
  over from a timeline, an earlier version or a transcript: find them in the picture (the difference
  between one frame and the next spikes on a cut). Everything that starts, ends or resets at a cut
  does it on that frame: a zoom, a title, a dim, a layer. One frame early or late shows a flash of
  the other take.

#### Retakes

Keep the best take of each thought, once. A mechanical pass can propose candidates
([first-pass.md](first-pass.md)) but never decides: it can't tell emphasis from a stutter, and it
misses retakes said in other words. Read the transcript yourself; finding nothing to cut is a
fine answer. When you describe a cut to the person, quote the words, not segment numbers.

A transcript smooths over retakes. Signs of a hidden one, to check by listening:

- a short word that lasts unusually long, over about 0.6 s (house): a stumble sits inside it;
- the same phrase twice, a few seconds apart;
- a word that makes no sense where it stands: two takes were heard as one sentence;
- a restart after "okay", "all right", a sigh or a clap.

For each candidate, in order:

1. **Is it really a retake?** Saying something twice on purpose isn't a mistake: a refrain, a
   list, emphasis, a correction the speaker wants heard ("two, no, three"). If so, leave it.
2. **Write out the complete thought that should survive**, in words, before you look at times.
   If you can't write it, you don't know yet what to cut.
3. **Pick the take that's whole, clear and meant.** Usually that's the last, not automatically:
   an earlier take wins when the later one adds a slip, drops a point or is cut short.
4. **Cut only the failed part.** Join two takes only where both sides are silent; if they run
   together, keep one whole take. Don't stitch unfinished pieces of several takes into a
   sentence nobody said.
5. **Keep words only one take has**, and the connecting words ("so", "and then", "but") that
   make the next sentence follow. Without them the thought after the join starts from nowhere.
6. **Read the joined sentence aloud in your head.** It must be one grammatical thought, with
   nothing said twice and nothing missing. If it isn't, go back to step 3. Fewer joins, all
   between phrases, beat a tighter cut.

Three examples (`/` marks a restart; before → after):

- Earlier take kept: "It runs every test in about two seconds. / It runs every test in, uh, a
  couple of, in two seconds." → "It runs every test in about two seconds." The later take adds
  a slip.
- Repetition that stays: "It's fast. Really, really fast." → unchanged. The second "really" is
  emphasis, though a detector calls it a stutter.
- Splice inside a sentence: "So when you deploy, the queue / the queue restarts on its own." →
  "So when you deploy, the queue restarts on its own." It works because there's a pause at the
  restart and the pitch, speed and loudness match on both sides, and it keeps "So when you
  deploy", which only the first take has. If the delivery doesn't match, the join is heard:
  keep the pieces whole instead.

#### Fillers

Two kinds, treated differently:

- **Always-safe sounds**: "um", "uh", "er", "ah", and their equivalents in the video's language
  ("euh", "ähm", "este"). They carry no meaning.
- **Context words**, which are sometimes meaning: "like", "so", "you know", "right", "actually",
  "I mean", "kind of", "basically". Test each one: without it, does the sentence still say the
  same thing, and does the speaker still sound like themselves? Two yeses, or it stays.

| Keep | Remove |
|---|---|
| "It works like a checklist." (a comparison) | "It's, like, really fast." |
| "The cache was stale, so the page was old." (a result) | "So… so the cache was stale." (first "so") |
| "You know the feeling when a deploy hangs." (asks the viewer) | "It's, you know, a queue." |
| "It actually passed." (against what was expected) | "Actually, um, let's open the config." |

Either kind goes only where there's silence on both sides and the line reads naturally without
it. A filler that runs straight into the next word stays: cutting it clips the words around it.
Never speed up a voice to save time.

A person's profile can say to leave fillers alone entirely: some creators never remove them
inside fluent speech. Removing them is a default, not a rule; see [TASTE.md](../TASTE.md).
