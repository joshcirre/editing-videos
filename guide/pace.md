### Set the pace from the content

Covers pauses, picture changes and caption size for each kind of stretch, and energy over time.
Everything here is a default that the person's profile or words override, except what's marked
`(hard rule)`: see [rules.md](rules.md).

Pace follows attention, not a clock: move on when the viewer has what the moment gives them.
Pick the row that fits each stretch; one video can change pace (a fast opening, a slow story).

| The stretch is… | Pauses kept | Picture changes | Captions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Made to stop a scroll (a short, a teaser) | 0.1–0.2 s; none at the start | every 1–3 s, each with a reason | 1–3 words at a time |
| Explaining or talking with someone | 0.3–0.5 s; longer before a key line | when there's something new to show; otherwise stay | 3–5 words |
| Speaking to a room, a story, something that should land | up to 1–2 s where the silence means something; keep the room's laughs and reactions | rarely; hold on the person | 4–6 words, or subtitles |
| Doing something on a screen | the screen sets it: cut the waiting (loading, typing), keep the result long enough to see | follow the action | 3–5 words |

The pauses are (house) starting points; the picture change for a short and the caption sizes are
(common).

Automatic silence and filler removal is a first pass. Put back the pauses that carry meaning,
and don't tighten a reflective moment to a feed's pace.

Across a long video, energy has a shape. The first minute and the last (house) can carry
punch-ins, big text and music hits. The middle is calm: the story carries it, the picture drifts
slowly or holds, and a screen stays still apart from what happens on it. A special shot (a
tilted pan across a screen, a full-frame graphic) is special two or three times in a video
(house); after that it's a habit.
