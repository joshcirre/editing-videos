### B-roll and visuals

Covers B-roll and visuals: when, where from, the real thing, screens filmed again, highlights.
Everything here is a default that the person's profile or words override, except what's marked
`(hard rule)`: see [rules.md](rules.md).

B-roll shows what's being said. It isn't decoration, and there's no quota: when the person is
the point (an emotional beat, a joke, a promise to camera), leave them on screen.

- **Anchor it to the word that names it.** It comes in at most 0.5 s (house) before that word
  and stays until the idea is finished, long enough to take in (about 1.5 s (house) or more).
  Where the edit already has a cut near there, or a sentence starts, start or end it on that
  instead of adding a new change; don't switch shots near the end of a sentence, and hold one
  shot across cuts inside the same idea.
- **Where it comes from, in order:**
  1. the video's own footage: other camera angles, screen recordings, earlier recordings, files
     the person handed over;
  2. real footage from a library with a clear license, if one is already set up;
  3. a diagram or graphic built from what was actually said;
  4. generated footage, only for what can't be filmed (an idea, a metaphor, the past), and only
     if a generator is already set up.

  With only the first and third, that's a complete edit. Where B-roll would help and nothing
  set up can supply it, leave the person on screen and list the moment with what to find.
- **Real beats generated.** Generated pictures are never shown as evidence that something
  happened, never stand in for real people, products or places as if real, and never carry
  numbers or results nobody said (hard rule).
- **Moving beats still.** A photo needs gentle motion (a slow push, about 2–3% (house) over the
  shot, about a fixed center, with no stepping) and stays short; prefer footage that moves on
  its own. Charts, calendars and timelines people need to read stay still.
- **Show the real thing.** The chart, post, article or page that's talked about, as it is: never
  redrawn by hand or swapped for a look-alike, even another from the same source (hard rule). Crop
  to the part that matters (headline, source, the passage) rather than shrinking the whole page.
- **A screen can be filmed again from its real source.** When the screen is a web page or an app
  whose code or address you have, the sharpest B-roll is that same page drawn again, not the
  recording enlarged: the real page, showing what the recording showed, laid exactly over where it
  sat in the frame. It must be the real page (its own code and styles), and every name and number on
  it is read from the recording, not from the live site, which may have moved on (hard rule). Follow
  what the person did: their scroll, their clicks, and one highlight for each thing they name, on
  the word that names it. Zoom by drawing the page larger, so the text stays sharp. Use accounts
  read-only, and keep keys and tokens out of anything you save (hard rule).
- **A highlight belongs to its shot.** (hard rule) It covers the whole word or element, and it is
  gone, fade included, before the shot changes. Left a few frames long, it sits on the wrong thing.
- **Use each clip once.** Another crop or speed of the same shot isn't a new shot.
- **Screens**: crop to the action and make it readable at the size people watch. Never show a
  whole page shrunk down.
- **Keep track** of where every clip came from and its license, and say which are generated
  (hard rule).
