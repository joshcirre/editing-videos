### Learning from notes

Covers turning a person's corrections into rules they never give twice, and matching how other
videos are edited. Everything here is a default that the person's profile or words override,
except what's marked `(hard rule)`: see [rules.md](rules.md).

When someone corrects an edit, change what the note asks for and nothing else, then ask whether
the note is a rule for next time. If it is, write it down with the note it came from, when it
applies and how to check it. An idea of your own (another sound, a new graphic) is offered in
words, not slipped into the revision. A show's or channel's own editing notes win over this
guide. When the editor you're in keeps the person's own keeps and cuts as learned habits, read
them first and follow them over this guide's defaults.

#### Read the note as one change

A vague note still asks for one thing. Make that one change, show it, and let them say if it
wasn't enough: three changes at once hide which one they wanted.

| They say | Change, and nothing else |
|---|---|
| "Feels rushed" | 0.2 s (house) more pause at every join in that stretch; no words back |
| "Feels slow" | 0.1 s (house) less pause at every join in that stretch; no words cut |
| "Too much going on" | one thing moves at a time: take out the layer that arrived last |
| "The text pops" | every title and its shade moves in and out over 0.3 s (house) |
| "It feels AI-made" | the breath back at every join, about 0.3 s (measured) |
| "The zooms are too much" | every zoom at 0.7× (house) its amount; same count, same timing |
| "I can't read it" | hold it as long as [text.md](text.md) says; if it does, 1.25× (house) larger |
| "That's awkward at 4:53" | look at the frames either side of the nearest cut first; fix that |
| "Too many graphics" | remove each one that only repeats the words; keep the evidence |
| "The cut is jumpy" | punch one side in 1.15× (common) or more, so the change looks meant |

`(measured)` here means measured on one creator's own cuts of two tutorials. Notes about sound
have their own table in [sound.md](sound.md).

- **A time in a note points at a cut.** Most "awkward" moments are a stray frame, a clipped
  word or an overlay that outlived its shot. Look and listen there before changing the pace.
- **Don't undo a note to satisfy a default.** If they said a beat is too long, a later pass
  doesn't put it back because a rule about coverage or pauses would.
- **Still unclear after looking?** Ask one question that names two readings ("shorter pauses,
  or fewer words?"). Don't guess twice.

#### A fix to one is a fix to all of that kind

When a note changes how one title, shade, zoom or highlight behaves, change every one of that
kind in the video and look at each. The person pointed at the one they noticed; the same
mistake is in the others, and they shouldn't have to find it again four minutes later.

#### Write it down

Every note goes in the notes ledger, `editing-notes.md` beside the project's `editing.md`, the
same day: one entry per note, in their words. The ledger is the history; the profile
([../TASTE.md](../TASTE.md)) holds the rules that came out of it.

```markdown
## 2026-03-14 · launch-recap, draft 2
> "the gradient cuts in and out"
- Changed: all 9 shades now slide in 0.4 s before the first word and out after the last.
- Rule: a shade slides in before its words and out after them, never with a cut.
  Saved to: editing.md (this show), under Rules.
- Check: stills across the entry and the exit of every shade.
```

A note that only fits this video ("lose the bit about pricing") ends `- Rule: one-off` and
needs no check. For the rest, decide where the rule lives:

- **One-off**: this video only. Ledger entry, nothing else.
- **This show**: the project's `editing.md`, with the note and the date.
- **Personal**: their own profile, once the same rule has been confirmed in two projects
  (house). Until then it stays with the show: one video's taste isn't yet theirs everywhere.

**Ask once, in one line, with your recommendation**: "Three notes this round. I'd save the
shade and highlight ones as rules for this show and treat the pricing cut as a one-off. OK?"
Batch the question at the end of a review round; asking after every small note is its own
annoyance. If you can't write files where you are, say each rule back in one line so they can
keep it.

#### A rule needs a check

Each rule you save says how to verify it next time: a still at an entry and an exit, a
measured gap, a scan of the render. Then the next edit proves the rule instead of remembering
it. A rule with no check gets broken quietly. Run these with the rest of
[check.md](check.md), and say in your handover which of their rules you checked and how.

| The note | The rule it became | The check |
|---|---|---|
| "There's a frame left in from a previous cut" | layers snap to the cut frame | scan for stray frames |
| "The highlight is still there after the shot moves" | gone before its shot changes | still at its last frame |
| "The gradient cuts in and out" | a shade slides in before the words, out after | stills across both |
| "The gradient goes over the face then behind it" | cutout covers the shade's whole time | still mid-entry |
| "No small label text above the headline" | one piece of text per beat | look at every title |

In full: every layer boundary snaps to the cut frame, checked by scanning the render for two
picture changes within a few frames of each other; a highlight is checked with a still at the
last frame of its shot; a shade with stills across its entry and its exit.

These five came from one finishing job. Each was given once.

#### Before the next edit

Read the ledger's rules and the profile before you touch anything. A note already given is
never something the person should have to spot again: if they do, the miss is yours, so add
the check that would have caught it. If one of this guide's defaults would undo something they
asked for, their rule wins, unless it's a hard rule ([rules.md](rules.md)).

#### Matching other videos

When someone wants their videos to feel like other videos (their own past edits, or a channel
they admire), study 3–6 (house) of them before editing and write what you find as rules with
numbers:
[study.md](study.md) says what to measure. Copy how the videos are edited, never their
footage, music or words (hard rule).

#### When the guide itself is wrong

If the same note comes from different people on different shows, it isn't taste: this guide's
default is off. Say so to the person and suggest the default should change, for the skill's
maintainers to decide. Don't edit the skill's files on your own; keep following each person's
own rule meanwhile.
