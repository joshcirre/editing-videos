### Several files of the same moment

Covers lining up cameras, mics and screen recordings of one conversation before the first cut.
Everything here is a default that the person's profile or words override, except what's marked
`(hard rule)`: see [rules.md](rules.md).

An offset that is wrong is wrong in every minute of the video: a voice out of step with its
mouth, a cut to someone a beat after they spoke. Settle where each file sits before you cut
anything, and say how you checked.

#### One room, or apart

Ask, or work it out from the sound, before any tool runs:

- **One room** (or one call recorded in one place): every mic hears every voice, loud or
  faint. The files share sound, and a tool can line them up by it.
- **Apart** (each person recorded on their own computer): each file has one voice and none of
  the other. Nothing in their sound matches, so a tool has nothing to find.

To tell: listen to one file, or measure its level, while the other person talks. A faint voice
there is one room. Silence is apart.

#### An automatic sync is a claim to check

A tool that lines files up by sound answers even when there is nothing to match. Check its
answer before you cut on it (hard rule). When it says how sure it was, read that, and treat a
weak match as no match. Then check with a turn, below, whatever it said.

#### When the sound can't say

- **Recordings that stopped together** (one call, ended for everyone at once): a file's offset
  is the longest file's length less its own. Started together is rarer: check before assuming it.
- **A screen recording with no sound**: find a word the speaker says as something appears
  ("now click Export") and the frame where it appears. The offset puts the two together.
- **Otherwise ask** for a moment both files have: a clap, a greeting, a slate.

#### Check with a turn

Find a question that ends on one mic and the answer that starts on the other. In step, the
answer starts after the question ends, less than a second after (house). An answer that starts
before its question ends, or a two-second gap in quick talk, is an offset that's off. Check one
turn near the start and one near the end: files from two devices can drift apart over an hour,
and one offset can't fix that. Say so when you find it; don't hide it by cutting around it.

For a camera, look as well: at a "p" or a "b", the lips are closed on the frame the sound starts.

#### Say it

In the hand-over, one line per file: its offset, how it was set (found by sound, worked out
from lengths, placed by a word on the screen, given by the person) and how you checked it. An
offset you couldn't check goes under Not checked ([handover.md](handover.md)).
